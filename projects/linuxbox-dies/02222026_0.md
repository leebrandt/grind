# Well, the Linux Box Shit the bed. And it may be my fault.

I have two computers I use regularly: my home computer, and my work laptop. My work laptop is a banger macbook pro (love that), but I've been in the process of trying to divest myself a little of the FAANG Mafia. I'm not gonna build a bunker and order a hundred cases of MREs... yet. But I just like the idea of supporting smaller businesses. Every one of the mega corps that exist today, was once a young start-up, out to topple *other* giants. The cycle never stops, eh?

Anyway, I digress.

I also have a decent monitor and sound set up for my computer, because I spend a lot of time at it, writing code, learning devops stuff, writing blog posts, and editing videos (maybe someday I'll actually edit and release one).

I have tried several docks and usb hubs in the past to allow the sharing of my nice monitor and music between my daily driver (a mini pc with Arch(btw) and Omarchy), and my work MBP. I started with a cheapy one, and tried a couple until I spent like $250 on one.All of them suck in different ways. The ~$100 one, did really well with the audio to usb, but the hdmi AND display port would cut off from time to time, or get "streaks" of pixels sometimes. But the $250 one doesn't seem to pass through the audio to the mac, so I have to change two USB-C cables two switch back and forth. It's only a minor PITA. 

The problem is: that nice monitor sits on a desktop shelf. The mini-PC and the dock sit *under* that shelf. I didn't realize it, but the way I had them sitting under the shelf, the dock was generating heat and the intake fan from the mini-PC was sucking it right up. Nice, hot exhaust, right in its face. When I went to bed, I left the computer on and sucking up the exhaust fumes of the dock all night. 

In the morning, the Linux box had freaked out a bit. Windows weren't closing, I was having to force-quit some processes. So I restarted. Which made it worse. 

## What I found out later...

The heat had caused the nvme drive to fail on a sector or to (BTRFS), and the OS booted in read-only mode. 

I'd never had that happen before, so I spent ALL of Saturday morning trying to figure out why my main computer wasn't working. Cursing the open-source Gods.

Yelling about, "No *wonder* Linux can't win on the desktop. Just random failures out of nowhere. *Screw* open source!"

I crashed-out (as the kids would say).

I'd had this whole Geekend(tm) planned of setting up a container and AI lab on my Big Machine and coding and working from a smaller machine. Now I'm trying to figure out why my Linux desktop isn't working.
