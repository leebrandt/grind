# Tracy Lee Brandt Sr.

My dad passed away last Tuesday. He'd been in rough shape for a while so it wasn't a shock, but I imagine it's never easy for *anyone* to get that news. I didn't understand what my aunt was saying at first when she called. What do you mean? Oh! You mean *passed*. Damn.

My dad was born in Spokane, WA in the late 40s. His father had an industrial accident that left him unable to work for quite awhile. My dad worked to help support the family, while finishing high school AND lettering in track. He was good at math and his classmates would often ask him for help with their math homework. He was the oldest of five (one brother and three sisters). His oldest sister, Gloria MacDonald remembered him best: "When he was working and helping my mom with food and bills, he would give his whole paycheck and keep only $10 for gas so he could get back and forth to work" and "sometimes he and I would have to leave school at lunch time and rush home to maybe put a roast in the oven so the oven for dinner that night because mom was at the hospital all day with my dad". He was certainly hard-wired to support people. To be a provider. 

He certainly was to me and my mom. They met in Los Angeles. My dad was in the Navy as an airplane mechanic stationed in San Diego and my mom was rooming with a girl who grew up across the street from him. He and a couple of other guys came up to LA one night and that's where they met. Shortly after that, they were married. Pretty quickly after that, I was born. My dad was at sea, so my mom was living with her parents. When he got out of the Navy, he worked several jobs before applying to Civil Service and getting a job at Warn Robbins Air Force base as an airplane mechanic, where he earned his Associate's Degree in Electrical Engineering. In 1979, he was offered a job at the China Lake Naval Weapons Center in Ridgecrest, CA to manage the group of airplane mechanics. To help better manage parts, he networked a bunch of computer terminals together so that the mechanics to remove parts from the electronic inventory as they used them. This was in the mid eighties, so he became the network administrator for the building they called "The Blue Whale". 

This is where I first learned about computers. We always had a terminal at home. My dad, who bought me the *150-in-1 Electronic Project Kit*, bought computer magazines and encouraged me to learn. He taught me how to throw a knuckle ball. He'd played some AAA baseball in GA when we lived there. He also taught me how to fish and hunt, how to fix a car, and how to always honor your word. He taught me that even though Dad gets the big piece of chicken, Dad eats last. He always had the shittiest car, the oldest clothes, and never the coolest toys. Everybody gets what they need, THEN he takes what's left. I am who I am, because of who he was. I am eternally grateful for all that he shared with me and I will miss him for the rest of my life. Rest easy, Dad. I've got it from here.
